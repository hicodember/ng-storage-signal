Natural gas data pipeline and research dashboard · Amber Foster

Does the weekly storage surprise move gas prices?

Every Thursday at 10:30 ET the EIA reports how much natural gas went into or out of U.S. storage. This pipeline pulls the storage and futures data, measures how far each report lands from normal for that time of year, turns that surprise into a trading signal on NYMEX natural gas futures, and tests it with trading costs, risk limits, roll-safe pricing, and a test period the model never saw while it was being tuned.

Methodology at a glance
  • Signal: storage change minus the seasonal norm (previous 5 years, same week plus or minus 2), divided by that season's usual variation (a z-score).
  • Roll-safe: every trade is priced on one contract, NG2 at entry if the front month expires mid-trade, so monthly roll jumps never count as profit or loss.
  • No look-ahead: entry at the release-day close, settings chosen on Jan 2012 to Dec 2018 only, then scored once on Jan 2019 to Apr 2024.
  • Realistic: 5.0 bps costs each way, volatility-based sizing, a 2.0x cap, and a 500-run random baseline.

Risk controls

  • Volatility targeting: each position is sized so it aims for 20% annual volatility, using the last 20 days of price moves.
  • Leverage cap: no position larger than 2.0x, even when markets are calm.
  • One position at a time: a new signal is skipped if the last trade is still open.
  • Costs: 5.0 bps charged on entry and on exit.

How I kept the test honest

  • No look-ahead: the "normal" change for each week uses only the 5 years before it. Entry is at the close on release day, so the report-day move is not counted.
  • Seasonal scaling: each surprise is divided by how much storage changes usually vary at that time of year (same calendar week plus or minus 2 weeks, previous 5 years), so a summer miss and a winter miss are judged on the same scale.
  • Roll-safe prices: the front-month futures series jumps every month when one contract expires and the next takes over. Every trade is priced on a single contract from entry to exit (contract 2 at entry when the front month expires mid-trade), so those jumps never count as profit or loss.
  • Out-of-sample test: settings were picked on data up to January 2019; everything after is scored once.
  • Luck check: compared with random trade directions using the same timing and costs.
  • Known limits: the expectation is a 5-year seasonal average, not the analyst survey traders actually use, so real surprises are sharper. Daily closes miss the intraday reaction at 10:30 ET. The roll calendar ignores exchange holidays, so a roll can be off by a day near a holiday.
  • Data window: EIA's free futures files stop on April 05, 2024, so the test runs through that date. Extending it needs a newer futures data source.

Next steps

  • Weather: add heating and cooling degree day forecasts (HDD/CDD). Weather forecast revisions drive more gas volatility than anything else, and they shape what the market expects before each report.
  • A sharper expectation: replace the 5-year seasonal average with the analyst consensus survey, so the surprise matches what traders actually react to.
  • Intraday timing: use minute data to measure the reaction right at 10:30 ET instead of at the daily close.
  • Regional basis: test whether surprises in specific regions move the spread between Henry Hub and hubs like Waha or Algonquin.
